Please do the following before emailing your ROP point person.

### A real UC Berkeley email address

Use your `@berkeley.edu` address and make sure your ID is professional (i.e. not catzoutofthebag\@berkeley.edu, but clancy.wilmott\@berkeley.edu).

### An email signature

Signatures are important pieces of information. Your signature should include:

- Your full name

- Your department/program + graduating year

- How people should contact you (i.e. your email)

- Any links to LinkedIn etc...

Learn how to make yours <a href="https://support.google.com/mail/answer/8395?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">here</a> (<a href="https://support.google.com/mail/answer/8395?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https\://support.google.com/mail/answer/8395?hl=en\&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop</a>)

Adding logos has pros and cons - they can increase visibility and institutional recognition, but they can also get caught in spam filters.

### A Google check

Quickly google your name and "UC Berkeley" and see what comes up. 

If what you see is not professional, the easiest way to drive those old photos down to the bottom of the search is to create new profiles on major sites such as LinkedIn, X, Instagram, and Github (this one you'll need to do this for class, anyway) - even if you don't use them.

### &#x20;LinkedIn (optional, but I do recommend)

LinkedIn has become the bare minimum for professional visibility, and it's a useful way to keep connected to people you have worked with.&#x20;

Make sure your LinkedIn has a photo too!
