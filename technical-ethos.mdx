title: How to Tech
---
A key component of this course is teaching you how to think like cartographers - not just theoretically and geographically, but also technically.

Based on research we have undertaken about teaching geospatial technical skills, we have learned that while step-by-step notebooks teach students skills in particular software quickly, this learning doesn't last and it is not adaptable to other contexts.

This course prioritises teaching *durable* skills which can be *adapted* to other software and workflows as the industry standards shift and change. A large part of this is *teaching you to* *teach* *yourself&#x20;*&#x6B;ey skills under our guidance, so that when you're on your own, you're able to do it confidently and fluently.&#x20;

For this reason, while I provide very specific instructions at times to undertake certain exercises, you'll also see a lot of links to "how to" websites and tutorials externally made. This is to help train you into figuring out what is already out there, for free, so you can then see how I have adapted it in the course. This is how we all learned to code in the early digital world, and it is absolutely doable!

But I know this can be scary - and that is why I make an agreement with you, and you will need to trust me. If you can rise to the challenge and face the potential for failure, confusion or uncertainty head on, I will *not* penalise you for being really bad at coding or math. We do not deduct points based on your failure to achieve a goal - we award points based on your successes and perserverence, and this is why we have the grading structure we do.&#x20;
