Welcome to being caught between mighty institutions - this is the stuff of outward facing work. We need to be careful here about what it means to work with clients, and make maps for other people.&#x20;

In this course, you are not only students - you are acting as emerging professional cartographers, researchers, and public communicators working in collaboration with a real client: California State Parks’ Reexamining Our Past (ROP) Initiative.

This means your work exists in multiple contexts at once:

- As a learning exercise

- As a public-facing institutional project

- As interpretive material that may shape how visitors understand history, race, colonialism, and Indigenous sovereignty

- As part of a relationship between UC Berkeley, your instructors, and California State Parks staff

Because of this, your role includes creativity, responsibility, professionalism, and ethical awareness.

### Key Principles for Client Facing Work

Working with clients means balancing creativity, accountability and working within someone else's framework. For this project specifically, working with a public institution means the guidelines are often stricter and have greater consequences. In democratic societies, public institutions don't just represent themselves, they represent the will of the people writ large. 

As you work through this project, there are a few key things to remember: 

- You are not  expressing your own ideas but instead you are helping communicate complex and sensitive ideas on behalf of other people who work within a broader institutional context.

- In this context, this is not just a design project. For the ROP, this is interpretative work combining public history with ethical responsibility.

- When you work professionally, projects move through revision, feedback, and authorization in order to ensure we stay on message and have quality control. This means that your draft is a contribution but it is not ready until it has hit a certain standard and been approved.

- Our job here is not to fix history, or provide our own interpretation - instead, our role is it is to contribute thoughtfully to collaborative public interpretation in conversation with ROP staff, and translate that into a cartographic framework.

- Mistakes happen! While we try to avoid them, the key for professionalism is to respond responsibly, and try to come up with solutions to present to your supervisor, rather than hiding them (they always come out).
